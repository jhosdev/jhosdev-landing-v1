# Design system & method

How design work happens in this repo. Two parts: the five patterns every strong site shares (distilled from Apple España, Lamborghini, Superhuman, Osmo, Slash, Auros, Structured), and this site's own answers to them. Any redesign session starts here.

## The five patterns (checks for every artboard/page)

1. **One idea, stated as a place.** Not a mood board — one room. Every later argument resolves against that sentence.
2. **Small palette, one accent, hoarded.** 8–20 named colors, almost all neutral. One accent element per screen; spending it twice destroys it. Surfaces are a 4–5 step ramp of one hue; dark/light bands pace the page instead of dividers.
3. **Two families, extreme size range, few weights.** A voice face for display plus a utility face. Hierarchy comes from big size jumps on a shared scale, not more weights. Display leading 0.84–1.0, body ~1.5.
4. **Imagery is everything or almost nothing.** Full-bleed photography/video, or one made object that does all the work. Never stock, illustration, or decorative icons.
5. **Motion and shadow barely specified.** One hover ease (~0.2s), one scroll-linked reveal, near-zero shadows; separation by surface color and whitespace. Pick the radius pair first and hold it everywhere — radius is the fastest tell between systems.

## This site's answers (the "Machine" system)

The home page and the article pages. The lab pages (`/lab/*`) keep the older terminal system they were sketched in; see the end of this section.

- **The place:** you are looking at the Machine's file on one person. A surveillance system (Person of Interest) has found the site owner, classified them as "the asset", and keeps the record open: who they are, who they are linked to, what they built, what they transmitted. Every section is a view of that file, and every motion is the Machine doing its job (acquiring, analysing, classifying, decoding), never decoration.
- **Palette:** the same near-black blue ramp (`#0b0e13` bg → `#0e1219` panel → `#1c232d` border → `#2e3947` hover) and text steps (`#5d6a79` decorative labels → `#7d8a99` small informational text → `#8d99a8` prose → `#c7d1dc` → `#f0f4f8`). Colour is classification, and each one means exactly one thing:
  - **white** `#f0f4f8`: unclassified. Default brackets, index tags, neutral verdicts.
  - **red** `#ff5c5c`: unidentified, or recording. The REC light, a tag before analysis, an error in the prompt.
  - **amber** `#ffc857`: under analysis / in progress. Tags mid-movement, an `active` project, focus-mode controls ("you are seeing an excerpt").
  - **cyan** `#6ee0ff`: the confirmed asset, and anything you can act on (links, the prompt, the selected entity, the current chip).
  - **green** `#57d9a3`: system nominal. Monitoring, a `production` project, a current employer, a decoded transmission, an open channel.
  A colour used for anything else is a bug, with one exception that is not a classification: the cat's coat, a tabby in a few warm steps (two stripe browns, the coat and its far step, ginger, cream and its far step: `COAT_RGB` in `draw.ts`) and her eyes in the system's green, used for her and nothing else. Tokens live in `src/styles/global.css`; the canvas reads the same values from `src/components/machine/draw.ts`.
- **Type:** IBM Plex Mono for everything the Machine says (titles, tags, labels, names, the prompt) and IBM Plex Sans for everything a person wrote (summaries, highlights, descriptions, article prose). Machine-speak is uppercase with tracking; content keeps its own case. The handle (the GitHub username from the resume, never `user@host`) is the one display size (up to 88px); section titles 22px; prose 14.5 to 16.5px.
- **The monitor (hero):** the asset section's feed is the Machine's monitor screen, in the DOM. Centre, one per line: the handle under the cyan lock, the role, the location, the full name. Left: stack telemetry (`content.stack`). Right: employment, in data order, each entry linking to its record. The side columns wrap by word and are never truncated; below 900px the subject sits on top and the two columns side by side under it. A `replay intro` control lives in the feed's bottom-right corner.
- **Language:** the Machine is not translated. Its vocabulary (HUD chrome, scene names, section titles, classification tags, command names) is English in every locale; only what is said to the visitor (helper sentences, help descriptions, accessible names) and the person's own content are translated. `copy.ts` keeps the two apart as `MACHINE` and `HUMAN`.
- **Imagery:** none, with one exception. The made objects are the camera feed around the name and the canvas layers (map, wires, scan bars, waveforms). The exception is the about section's subject photo (`meta.site.about.photo`), filed as evidence: a 4:3 print under corner brackets, captioned in machine-speak (`SUBJECT PHOTO · <cat>`, `FILED <build date>`), with a dashed lead down to the particle frame it is the source for. A real lazy `<img>` with its alt text from the data; without the field nothing renders.
- **Shape:** square corners everywhere, and no borders where brackets can do the job: a thing the Machine has locked onto gets four corner brackets and a filled classification tag above its top-left corner. Shadows: none (the command output uses a solid outline of the background colour to separate itself).
- **Grammar of a section:** index tag + title + hairline + a short readout on the right, one sentence in sans under it, then the content. New sections reuse this (`SectionHead.astro`) and the bracket/tag primitives (`.mc-lock`, `.mc-tag`).
- **Motion:** this is a motion site now, with JavaScript and canvas. The rules that keep it honest:
  1. **Content first.** Every section is server-rendered semantic HTML that reads correctly with JavaScript off. Canvas is `aria-hidden` and never carries information. Text that "decodes" keeps its real text in the DOM the whole time; the scrambled frame is painted over it from an attribute.
  2. **One intro, once.** A ~11s cut of the authored timeline (boot, sweep, acquire, identify) plays once per tab, never with a hash in the URL, and any key, tap or scroll ends it immediately. A visible `skip` button (bottom right, 44px, focused when the intro starts) does the same, and `replay intro` in the hero plays it again. In ACQUIRE a cat made of particles materialises out of the Machine's dot grid, moves, and its particles resolve into the handle; which cat is a variant (see "Intro variants").
  3. **One movement per section**, about three seconds, played when the section scrolls into view or is navigated to: asset = acquired and confirmed, then the telemetry and employment columns report in; about = a second entity in the frame, a sphere of particles analysed until it resolves into the cat; associations = wired to the asset one per beat; projects = scanned and classified; open source = indexed one by one on a commit rail; writing = intercepted and decoded; contact = handshake to open channel. An article page gets one short entrance (under two seconds), then nothing moves.
  4. **Pure functions of time.** The intro (`window.seek(seconds)`, `?seek=`) and every movement (`?move=0..1`) render from a time value, so any frame can be reproduced, captured or tested.
  5. **Quiet when idle.** After a movement only ambient canvas motion remains (map, packets, carrier), at half frame rate, paused off screen. Device pixel ratio is capped at 2.
  6. **`prefers-reduced-motion: reduce`:** no intro, no movements, no loops. Content is simply present and the canvases draw one settled frame.
- **Sections:** asset, about, associations, projects, open source, writing, contact (the personal file comes right after the asset). Open source and about come from `meta.site.openSource` / `meta.site.about` and are not rendered when the data lacks them. Open source is a listing, not a grid of targets: rows on a commit rail, no panels and no brackets, each linking out to its repository. About is the personal file: paragraphs, known facts, and, beside them, the subject photo (when the data has one) above the cat's frame.
- **The cat:** Luna, the owner's cat (her name, and the fact that there is a cat, come from `about.facts`; no other fact about her is in code). One drawing everywhere: a tabby made of particles, never a flat fill. `catrig.ts` (pure maths) turns a pose (spine, four feet, head, tail, ears, eyes, a yawn, a head tilt, a tail wrapped round toward the camera) into a shape: body, legs and tail are discs swept along smooth curves with tapering radii, joined in a distance field by a smooth minimum; the skull and ears are closed outlines that turn from profile to front. Her particles are scattered once, at rest, by seeded dart throwing (blue noise: by chance, but never clumped), denser along each part's outline than inside, and each keeps its place in its part's own coordinates (how far along, how far across as a fraction of the half width; on the head an angle and a fraction of the way to the outline, laid out on the face seen from the front), so a moving cat never boils. Every particle has its own size and brightness from skewed draws (mostly small, a few large and bright, the big ones round); a few strays sit just outside the outline so the edge is never a drawn line; where a near leg crosses the body its outline shows as brighter dots. Parts in front hide the dots behind. The face is drawn after her photo: a round, broad head with a short muzzle, wide-set ears with softly rounded tips, a forehead M and cheek lines, pale whisker pads and chin, a small dark nose. Her eyes are lines, sleepy and sweet: open, a dark upper lid with a sliver of green under it (the photo's half-shut eyes); shut, a short pale curve. (Rings read as goggles, almonds as a mask, points stared, a slit pupil looked hard; lines won.) A dark mark (stripes, the M, the nose) reads on the dark ground by being dim: its dots step down a brightness level. The bib is a small pale patch at the throat; the chest below it is ginger with a tabby's necklaces. How many particles, how they gather, their sizes and brightness and whether they streak is a *look* (`STYLES` in `cat.ts`: sparse, varied, cloud, detail, soft, motion, haze); the site uses `LOOK` (haze: cloud and soft at once, a dense core of fine particles that carries the form, its outline a step brighter, under a veil of faint specks, each riding at a fixed offset on a particle of the core so it costs next to nothing to place, which frays the edge), a one-word change, and `/lab/` shows every look on the same pounce. The coat is the one place warm colour appears (see Palette); before the Machine has classified her she is brightness only, on its grey ramp. `catkeys.ts` is her repertoire: named poses (asleep in a loaf, drowsy, a yawn, standing, the front stretch, sitting with the tail round her paws) and timelines that ease between them, the head a moment ahead of the body and the tail a moment behind, a foot that has to move lifting and stepping instead of sliding. **About** (the personal file, a 20s loop, `?luna=<s>` freezes it): a sphere of noise resolves into her asleep and grey, a second scan pass classifies her (coat and eyes come on behind the line), then she wakes, yawns, gets up into a long stretch, sits by the file and looks back at it, turns to you with a slow blink, lies down and sleeps again, while the Machine logs what she is doing in the frame's corner (`catLog` in `copy.ts`); the Machine's camera reframes on her, closer as she curls up, wider as she stretches. Reduced motion: still, asleep. **The command** (her name, or `cat`): she forms over the hero looking back over her shoulder, turns to you, yawns, gives you a slow blink, and goes (`?cat=<s>` freezes it). White brackets and a white tag: an entity the Machine has seen, not classified as anything else.
- **Intro variants:** the cat phase of ACQUIRE is selectable with `?intro=<name>` (it always replays, and combines with `?seek=`); `/lab/` shows them side by side (`catphase.ts`, the intro's own drawing code), each with a caption, its added time and a link to the full intro. Every one opens the way the boot does: a quiet panel of dots (the Machine's 28px grid), then dots of that grid light up and travel to their places on her, outline first, in about a second; she is grey, and the first pass of the amber scan line classifies her, her coat and green eyes coming on behind it; then she moves, and the same particles resolve into the handle. `nap` (the default, `DEFAULT_INTRO`): asleep in a loaf; as the scan line passes her ears one of them flicks; she lifts her head, her eyes open green (the system's nominal) and she gives the camera a slow blink; +1.1s. `pounce`: crouched, she watches the red marker (the square the sweep put on the subject), the rear wiggles, she squeezes down and jumps, the forepaws land on it and the ears flinch; +1.6s. `stretch`: lying awake, she yawns at the scanner, then gets up into a long front stretch with the tail held high; +1.3s. `walk`: mid-stride she walks on, stops, lowers herself into a sit and looks back with a nod; +1.8s. A planted foot is computed from distance travelled (walk) or steps when a pose moves it (keyed poses), the pounce is keyed poses eased without overshoot, and no particle jumps from one frame to the next (`tests/catrig.test.ts`). `sit` and `silhouette` were retired: `nap` does the sitting cat's look-at-you with her own character, and the scan that classifies her replaces the outline trace.
- **The invitation:** above the asset heading, a real link dressed as a button (dashed cyan border, 44px, a filled cyan `PLAY →`) invites to the play page with the one sentence said to the visitor; the strip is centred in the column, and the Machine's name inside the sentence is in its own face and colour (mono, uppercase, cyan, between brackets, no fill, so it is not a second button). Its icon is a tiny board that plays itself: six moves, then the Machine's three in a row struck in green; hovering plays it faster and nudges the arrow. Reduced motion shows the finished board, still. It is the only invitation: the home has no simulation section.
- **Navigation:** sections are addressable (`/#projects`, `/#writing`, `/#projects/<name>`). A hash opens *just that section* (focus mode) with an amber way back to the full file. The docked command line takes section names as commands, with a ghost suggestion and Tab completion; the chips above it do the same for people who will not type.
- **The play page** (`/play/`, `/es/play/`): tic-tac-toe against the Machine, in the Machine's frame (top bar, section head, brackets, a way back). The home keeps only the invitation; its `simulation` chip and the `simulation` / `play` commands go there.
- **Lab pages** (`/lab/`, `/lab/machine/`, `/lab/shell/`, `/lab/tictactoe/`) are the experiments this system came from. They keep the terminal chrome (`$ prompt` lines, green dot, 6px radius) and are not linked from the site. The tic-tac-toe panel is reused on the play page with its corners squared.

## Briefing a design agent

Give it: the north-star sentence (the Machine's file on the asset), the neutral ramp, the five classification colours with their single meanings, the two families and who speaks in which, square corners + brackets + tags, the six motion rules, then the specific task. Run an "AI tells" audit pass on any finished design and remove one decoration before shipping. Useful session tools when available: the design canvas skill for side-by-side directions (stable names A/B/C, main = leading candidate), aesthetic-direction references for naming the moves, deterministic checkers for contrast/padding.

Checking motion: stills cannot judge it. Freeze frames with `?seek=`, `?intro=`, `?move=` and `?cat=`, and drive a real browser for anything that depends on the clock, scrolling or typing.

## Rules

- New sections reuse the section grammar (index tag, title, readout, one sentence) and the bracket/tag primitives. No new visual primitive, and no new colour meaning, without updating this file first.
- Every piece of motion must be explainable as something the Machine is doing to the content. If it cannot be, it is decoration: cut it.
- Zero personal facts in code. Names, employers, projects, skills and articles come from the resume data and the writing collection; machine-speak and UI strings live in `src/components/machine/copy.ts` (the Machine's vocabulary once, in English; what is said to the visitor per locale). Even the cat's command is its name from the data.
- Any experiment (e.g. the tic-tac-toe panel on the play page) lives inside the system's frame and obeys the palette; it never introduces its own colours.
- Article prose stays plain: sans, max-width 720px, nothing moving after the entrance.
